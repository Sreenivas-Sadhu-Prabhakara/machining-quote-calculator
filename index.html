How it works

The calculator prices a machined part the way a job shop builds up a cost sheet — one rung at a time, so every rupee or peso in the quote is traceable.

Material is the raw stock for one piece. Machine time is your machine rate per hour multiplied by the cycle time, converted from minutes to hours (rate × cycle ÷ 60). Setup is a one-time cost — rate times setup minutes — spread evenly across the whole lot (÷ quantity), so a bigger order carries less setup per piece.

Those three make the works cost. Overhead is added as a percentage of works cost to cover power, consumables, tooling wear and shop admin, giving the factory cost. A scrap uplift then grosses the cost up — dividing by (1 − scrap%) — so the pieces you ship pay for the ones you reject. Finally margin is added on cost, and the per-piece price is multiplied by quantity for the lot total.

Assumptions & rates

Every rate is your own input — there is no fixed table of prices.

Setup is a one-time cost amortised across the quantity: the setup charge is divided by the number of pieces, so it shrinks per piece as the lot grows. A blank or zero quantity is treated as a lot of one for this step, so the cost never divides by zero.

The machine rate is an all-in hourly rate for the machine and operator. Cycle and setup times are entered in minutes and converted to hours by dividing by 60.

The scrap uplift grosses the cost up by dividing by (1 − scrap% ÷ 100), so the accepted pieces cover the cost of the rejects. A scrap figure of 100% or more is capped at 99.9% so the price stays finite.

Overhead is a percentage of works cost (material + machine + setup). Margin is applied on cost — the scrap-adjusted cost — not back-calculated from a selling price.

Amounts are shown in the currency you pick (₹ INR or ₱ PHP). Nothing is converted between currencies; switching currency only changes the symbol and formatting.
